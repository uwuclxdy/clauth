# Auto-switch

An ordered chain of accounts clauth hops down when the active one runs out of headroom. Opt-in: an account outside the chain is never switched to or away from, and an empty chain means clauth never switches on its own.

Edit the chain on the Fallback tab, as `fallback_chain` in `profiles.toml`, or over the REST API (the `--listen` route table on the [Daemon](Daemon) page). Codex profiles have a separate chain of their own ([below](Auto-Switch#codex)).

## The decision

On every scheduler tick, and once at startup:

1. The active account has to be a chain member. Nothing happens otherwise.
2. It has to be exhausted or dead.
3. clauth walks the chain in your `walk_order` (by default from the slot after it, wrapping) and switches to the first member with headroom.

The walk prefers members whose usage was read live over ones showing cached numbers, and falls through to accept a stale-reading member rather than strand you on an exhausted account.

The active account's own exhaustion is judged only on fresh readings, so a rate-limited poll cannot trigger a switch by itself. Two states are the exception, because neither can recover that evidence on its own: a dead account switches away on any reading, and an account whose usage polls have been rate-limited long enough to stop draining switches away too. A deep-stuck account with a last-known window still moves only when that window says it is genuinely spent; one with no window moves because the reading channel can never supply the missing exhaustion evidence. The same rule applies to Anthropic usage polls and to a provider's own usage endpoint.

An account clauth cannot measure gets no reading at all: one that holds only a long-lived setup token, with no OAuth login behind it and its usage probe off, since `/usage` needs that login's scope. The walk counts it as having headroom, and three paths reach it. The exhaustion walk takes it only once no freshly read member is clear, because it tries the freshly read ones first; a spent usage-credit cap keeps a member out of that pass while its `credit gate` is on, as surely as a crossed window does, so window room alone is not enough. `clauth start --auto` picks the same way and so reaches it in the same place: it prefers a member whose reading is fresh in its first pass over the chain and accepts any freshness in its second, where an account with no reading counts as clear. The hop off a crossed per-model week does not: when one model's week is past its line on an otherwise-healthy active, clauth walks the chain in your `walk_order` for the first member that is clear, with no freshness pass at all, and an account with no reading reads clear. So that hop can land a session on an unmeasured account while a freshly read one with room sits later in the walk. Once it is active, nothing reads it as exhausted, so clauth won't switch away from it on its own. `clauth login <name>` adds an OAuth login beside the setup token, which gives clauth a reading while sessions keep running on the setup token, and the confirm that login shows says so. That holds for a plain setup token. With `rolling_token` set, every rotation re-stamps the token from the stored login instead, so the sessions move onto the login you just made at the next rotation, another account's if that is what you logged in as. On an account clauth cannot measure the flag has nothing to roll from until that login exists, so it is this login that starts it, and the confirm and the note after it say that case instead. The other way out needs no login: turn on the account's `usage_probe` ([Configuration](Configuration#account-types)), and clauth reads it off the setup token itself, with every probe able to start its 5-hour window. The marks below go with the switch: a probe that fails is a failed read on the Usage row, not an account clauth has no way to measure. The Fallback rows and the Overview's chain rows mark such a member `◆` in the warning color (the Overview's account rows never carry `◆`: the `⊖`, `×` and `⊘` they do show are their own marks, for a canceled subscription or an organization's refusal, a broken login, and a setup token expired, mis-filled or refused), its Fallback card reads `can't read usage` and names what being unmeasured costs, adding one on the Fallback tab asks first and names the fix — unless that add also mixes api-key and OAuth accounts, when the mixing confirm shows instead and carries no fix — and the daemon names them in its log when it starts.

## Exhausted

An account is exhausted when either window is past its line.

| Window | Line | Set by |
|--------|------|--------|
| 5h | `95%` | per account, Fallback tab `rotate at` / `fallback_threshold` |
| 7d aggregate (else a monthly-only account's 30d) | `98%` | Config tab `weekly limit`, per account `weekly at` / `weekly_threshold` |
| 7d, per model | same as aggregate | the same values, checked per model window |
| 7d hard cap (else a monthly-only account's 30d) | `100%` | not configurable |

The weekly lines are deliberately below 100. Topping out a week bricks an account for days rather than hours, so clauth moves off while there is still room to land the hop. The 100% hard cap blocks an account regardless of every toggle below.

API-key accounts are judged on the same lines, using whatever 5h / 7d windows their provider publishes — Z.ai and MiniMax today — plus Alibaba's monthly quota as the long window. Before, only OAuth accounts could ever be exhausted, so a `fallback_threshold` on an api-key member never fired. A window a provider does not publish simply has no line to cross, and a best-effort scan of an unrecognised endpoint never counts: its numbers are guessed from the response shape, and parking an account on a guess is worse than not switching. An unmarked month-long bar (z.ai's MCP web-tools ceiling) renders as a bar but is not judged — the weekly line reads the 7d window, else Alibaba's monthly quota, so an Alibaba account with only a monthly reading still has that line to cross.

Per-model weekly windows (a "7d fable" window, say) gate the same way: an account whose scoped week is past the line stays out of rotation, since a session of the capped model landed there would strand, and the walk cannot know which model your next session runs. A session that has not started yet is the one case where the model *is* knowable, which is what [`start --auto`](Auto-Switch#choosing-where-a-session-starts) uses.

Three per-account toggles relax this:

- **`weekly gate`** off: ignore the soft weekly line for this account. The hard cap still blocks.
- **`scoped gate`** off: keep rotating to this account for other models, ignoring its capped per-model weeks. Blunt by nature — it drops the gate for every model, so a session of the capped model can then land here too. `start --auto` narrows the same judgment to the models a session will actually run, and needs no toggle.
- **`credit gate`** off: let auto-switch move onto this account after its usage-credit cap is spent. The cap refuses a credit-billed model (Fable, plus any family Claude Code's flag adds) and a launch whose model clauth cannot tell — none named, one it cannot place, or an agent launch that names no model — and both refusals happen whatever this gate says; a session launched on a named opus or sonnet model still runs there. It never moves the global account off the chain by itself. An account that serves inference on its own endpoint with its own key never spends these credits, so neither the cap nor this gate reaches it. A `--with-fallback` session launched on a credit-billed model, or already refused once for a spent cap, passes over this account whatever the gate says ([Running it](Auto-Switch#running-it)). A credit refusal on record ([Running it](Auto-Switch#running-it)) passes over the account that refused and no other: another account's spent cap is still judged by its gate. The recovery after a switch-off reads the gate only to prefer a member clear of a spent cap: it takes the home account back on its 5h and weekly windows alone, and when no member is clear, its last pass ignores the cap, so it can re-arm onto a spent one. The daemon log says once when an account's cap turns spent and once when it clears, judged on fresh readings; an account the usage probe reads once its OAuth pair is removed lets a spent cap go without a line. On an account the usage probe reads, the gate does nothing at all: a probe reading carries no credit figures, so its cap never reads spent, and only a refusal keeps a credit-billed session or the global account off it ([Running it](Auto-Switch#running-it)).

## Excluded members

The walk skips a member for any of these, worst first. The Overview and Fallback tabs render the reason on the account's row, all but `credits refused`, which the Usage card shows.

| Reason | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| `disabled` | you ran `clauth disable <name>`, or flipped it on the Setup tab |
| `canceled` | the subscription reads canceled at Anthropic |
| `org refused` | the account's organization refuses it to the usage probe: on hold, disabled, a key whose creator left it, an endpoint closed to its region or an address outside its allowed range; no login fixes it |
| `auth broken` | a refresh was rejected for good; the login needs `clauth login <name>` (a codex chain: `clauth login <name> --codex --browser`) |
| `key rejected` | a third-party provider rejected the inference api key; re-enter it on the Setup tab |
| `token rejected` | the server refused the account's setup token to the usage probe; re-mint it with `clauth login <name> --setup-token` |
| `logged out` | the account holds no login at all; log in with `clauth login <name>`, or add an api key on the Setup tab |
| `weekly spent` | 7d at 100%, dead until the week resets |
| `30d spent` | a monthly-only account's 30d quota at 100%, dead until it resets |
| `claude code blocked` | the messages limiter keeps refusing this account, twice running, with quota still ahead |
| `extra usage spent` | out of subscription quota and out of the spend ceiling below |
| `5h <pct>%` | 5h past its line |
| `<model> <pct>%` | one model's week past its line, per the `scoped gate` above |
| `credit cap spent` | the usage-credit cap is spent while its `credit gate` is on; credit-billed models (Fable) fail there, other models still run |
| `credits refused` | the account refused a credit-billed request, and since then no request on it has succeeded and no window reset has left it headroom; passed over for a launch that may need credits, for a session that may need credits until nothing else is left, and by the global account's walk until nothing but a spend-armed account, a spent `last_resort` account, a spent account or a switch-off is left, while other models still run. The Usage card shows it, the row does not ([Running it](Auto-Switch#running-it)) |
| `weekly <pct>%` | past the weekly line, per the `weekly gate` above |
| `30d <pct>%` | a monthly-only account's 30d quota past the weekly line |

Being dead is its own switch trigger. An active account marked `auth broken`, `key rejected`, `token rejected`, `canceled`, `org refused`, or `claude code blocked` is walked off instead of wedging on the corpse, and a rejected-key or rejected-token member is never picked as a target or as switch-off recovery. Alibaba's lapsed console login is different: it suppresses only the usage poll because the api key can still serve, so the account stays in the chain.

## Last resort and preferred

Two radio toggles on a member's Fallback card. Marking one clears it on every other member, and no member can hold both.

- **`last resort`** is the parking spot: chosen only once every other member is past its line, and left only for your preferred account or, by a session that needs usage credits, when its credits are spent. Claude Code then surfaces its own out-of-limit message when that account runs dry too.
- **`preferred`** is the home account. Once it reads clear and fresh, clauth walks back to it on its own, from wherever the chain left you.
- **`walk order`** (Config tab) reorders each accept pass among the members it already accepts. Ties keep chain position, every other gate is unchanged, and the default `chain` is today's walk exactly.
  - `soonest weekly reset`: the member whose 7d window (a monthly-only account: its 30d quota) resets soonest drains first, so less quota expires unspent; a member with no readable reset ranks last. With no `preferred` set, a healthy active walks home to the soonest-reset clear+fresh member and parks there.
  - `most weekly headroom`: a hop lands on the member whose 7d window (a monthly-only account: its 30d quota) is least used, 5h breaking a tie, so the week spreads across the chain instead of always landing on the next slot. A window past its reset or with no reset yet counts as unused, and so does a missing 5h window; a member with no weekly window to read ranks last, and a healthy active never moves for it.

Which account is home can also depend on the day. A `preferred_days` list in a profile's `config.toml` ([Configuration](Configuration#configtoml)) names the weekdays that account is home, in local time, and those days are claimed against every account: a plain `preferred` elsewhere stands down on them and holds the rest. Only chain members that could actually serve are home at all: a list on an account that is off the chain, disabled, auth-broken, carrying a rejected api key or setup token, or logged out is inert, so it never leaves a day with nobody home, and a plain `preferred` on such an account is inert too rather than marking a homecoming the walk cannot make. One line on the weekend account is the whole weekday / weekend split, and the rollover needs no restart. The list reaches the claude chain only — the codex chain has no home account, so `preferred_days` does nothing there.

## When everyone is out

With no `last resort` member, the chain-global `quota spent` setting decides:

- `stay on active` (default) keeps you on the last account, so Claude Code shows its own limit message.
- `switch off all` clears the live credentials and unsets the active account. It re-arms automatically onto the first member that recovers.

## Burn-aware switching

Config tab `switch mode`, default `static`.

`static` compares utilization to the threshold. `burn-aware` instead projects utilization forward from your recent burn rate and switches once the projection would cross 100% before the next refresh. Heavy burn moves you early; light burn rides past the threshold toward 100%.

| Knob | Default | Effect |
|------|---------|--------|
| `burn floor` | `98%` | the earliest utilization burn-aware may switch at |
| `burn horizon` | `60s` | how far ahead it projects, capped by the refresh interval, or by the probe interval on an active account the usage probe reads |

The rate is a recency-weighted fit over the last hour of samples in `usage_history.jsonl`, ignoring idle gaps and reset boundaries. It needs three samples to compute anything; an account without them falls back to its static threshold. Burn-aware can only switch earlier than static, never later.

## Extra usage (real money)

Off by default, and three separate things must all be true before a dollar is spent:

1. Config tab `allow extra usage` is `pay-as-you-go`.
2. That account's `max spend` ceiling is above $0.
3. Billing is actually enabled on the account at Anthropic.

An account with subscription quota left always outranks one that costs money, and a `last resort` member that still serves for free outranks a paying one. clauth arms against 90% of the smaller of your ceiling and the account's own limit, leaving margin for the gap between polls. An account whose spend it cannot read is never armed.

The ceiling is a real stop, not just a gate on starting: once the account has spent it, clauth stops using that account. It parks on your `last resort` member if you named one; otherwise the `extra usage spent` setting decides, defaulting to switching off all accounts. That default is the opposite of `quota spent` on purpose, since staying put costs nothing when quota runs out and costs money when a budget does.

An armed account with `extra usage spent` set to `stay on active` and no `last resort` member in the chain can spend without a stop. clauth marks that on the card and the daemon warns about it at boot.

## Keeping the chain warm

An account's 5h window opens on its first real request, so a chain member you have not touched starts its clock only when the chain lands on it — and then holds you there for a full five hours before it resets. `auto_start` ([Configuration](Configuration#auto-start-the-5-hour-window)) opens that window ahead of time with a one-token ping, and the shared queue ([Configuration](Configuration#interleaving-it-across-accounts)) spaces those opens `5h / N` apart across the accounts that opted in, so whichever member the chain hops to has usually been cycling already and resets sooner. Both are independent of the chain itself: auto-start never switches anything, and the walk never consults it.

## Running it

The chain runs wherever the decision loop runs: an open TUI, or `clauth daemon` with the TUI closed ([Daemon](Daemon)). Only one of them decides at a time.

`clauth start <profile> --with-fallback` gives a single session its own chain, so that session hops accounts while your global one stays put. It needs a running daemon and an OAuth account inside a chain that holds a second member to move to, and it does not work alongside `--isolated` ([Quickstart](Quickstart#rules-worth-knowing)). On macOS the swap also writes the session's per-config-dir Keychain item, so the running session follows the chain there too. A swap onto a member that runs on a setup or rolling token, which has no refresh token, removes that item instead, so the session reads its new login from that member's token file; the MCP-server logins the item held are saved into that file first, so the session keeps them. Claude Code can still put such a login back into the item later: its first save of credentials inside the session (an MCP sign-in or refresh) copies the whole blob it read from the token file, the bearer included, into the item, and from then on the session reads that copy. When the member's rolling token rotates, the old bearer stops working, so right after re-stamping the member's token file clauth replaces that exact copy in every running session's item with the new bearer and leaves everything else in the item as it was. An item that holds anything else, a login with a refresh token, a setup-token mint or another account's bearer, is left alone. A background `--with-fallback` session — launched with `-- --bg`, or moved to the background from its terminal — keeps following the chain after its terminal is gone: the daemon adopts it on its next tick and holds it while a Claude Code job names its tree, and a daemon stopped while that job still runs hands it on to the next daemon. With no daemon running it waits, handed off, until one starts; a `clauth switch <sid> <profile>` on it then fails with `session '<sid>' is a background session no clauth daemon holds yet`. A daemon refuses to adopt a session whose Claude Code refreshed its own login while no daemon held it, since nothing proves whose refresh chain that login is: the session keeps running on its own login without following the chain, the daemon logs the refusal and retries, and the switch keeps failing the same way.

Such a session also leaves an account whose usage-credit cap is spent when it runs a credit-billed model: at once when it was launched on one (its `[models]`, `--model`, `ANTHROPIC_MODEL` or a settings `model`), otherwise on its own first refusal from Claude Code (`You've hit your monthly spend limit.`). From then on it skips every account whose cap is spent, whatever that account's `credit gate` says. The leave itself may land on a member whose `[models]` differ from the account the session started on, which every other hop skips: the swap moves the credentials only, so the session keeps the model it launched with. An account that serves inference on its own endpoint with its own key never reads as capped this way. A session running opus or sonnet stays where it is.

An account that refused a credit-billed request is on record for it in its `credit_refusal.json`, whichever session was refused: a `clauth start` session, a delegate, a `claude --bg` job running in a clauth runtime, or a bare `claude` session on the global account. It is the account's one refusal state: a session refused there reads the same record as every other session. From then on every walk passes it over for a session whose launch names a credit-billed model or that was refused for credits itself, and for a `clauth start --auto` launch that may need credits, whatever its reading says: a reading the usage probe took carries no credit figures, and a measured one can read uncapped while Claude Code refuses. The global account's walk passes it over too, in its headroom and home passes. Its passes for `last_resort` accounts with headroom and for spend-armed accounts, which serve every request, read the chain's order. After a `last_resort` account with headroom it takes the refusing account, since that still serves what its plan covers for free: ahead of a spend-armed account, parking on a spent `last_resort` account, staying on a spent account or signing every session out. A session that may need credits takes it only after every other pass, one with headroom first and a `last_resort` account only after, rather than stay on a spent account; its next refusal walks it off again. A session whose launch names no credit-billed model clauth can see can still land on it, until it is refused for credits there itself. The record holds across a restart, across a change of the clauth process that fetches usage, across the refused session's end and across a `/clear` in it. It goes with any later successful turn clauth places on that account, whatever model served it and in whichever session: a plan-covered turn proves nothing about the credits, but it brings back an account that refused once, and if the credits are still spent the next credit-billed request is refused once more and walks the session off again. Later means written after the refusal in the transcript it came from, whatever the clock said, while clauth still knows where that line lies in the file; otherwise, and in any other transcript, stamped after it. Time does not clear it, since clauth does not know the date the cap resets; a fresh reading that shows one of its windows reset after the refusal, with the account back under its threshold and weekly line, does, and no refusal from before that reset comes back on record; a login as another account drops it along with the old one, for good: no refusal from before that login is recorded again, not even by a clauth process that takes over fetching usage and reads the refused session anew. A re-minted setup token and a login that proves no account keep it, since neither names another account. clauth notes which account the refusal came from, so a later read of the profile's account that names another one drops it too, even after such a login. A clear holds the same way: neither the refusal it cleared nor one stamped before the turn that cleared it comes back on record, while one another session recorded after that turn stays. The Usage card reads `credits refused`, with how long ago, and the daemon log says once when a refusal comes on record and once when it clears. An account that never refused is judged by its reading, probed or measured alike.

The global account serves your bare `claude` sessions; a `clauth start` session runs on its own profile, and a global switch never moves it. clauth places a refusal off the one live process that holds the conversation: on the account of the session whose process it is, or whose process it descends from, as a `clauth start` session's child or a delegate's does; on the account of the session whose runtime tree the Claude Code job running the conversation names, for a `claude --bg` job; otherwise, for a process whose record sits in `~/.claude/sessions`, on the global active as `~/.clauth/profiles.toml` names it, so a switch another clauth process made counts from the next read. A process started with a login of its own (`CLAUDE_CODE_OAUTH_TOKEN`) runs on that login's account, so its refusal lands on no one, and so does one from a process Claude Code's background service runs whose job clauth cannot find. Two live processes on one conversation place nothing, and neither does a process two sessions' rows both hold. A refusal clauth places on no account moves no session either: the session stays where it is and is refused again on its next credit-billed request. A refusal is placed on the global active only if it came after the session's own start (its record's `startedAt`), so a resumed conversation's old refusal lands on no one, and neither does any refusal from a session whose record names no start. Within 30s of a global switch a refusal lands on no one either, since it may answer a request sent before the switch. The same goes for the first 30s after a clauth process first starts fetching usage and for anything before them: that process reads a running session's transcript whole, and an older refusal may have answered an account that was active before it. With no live process left on the conversation, a `claude --bg` job's record names the tree it runs in, an `--isolated` session's transcript names its account by the tree it sits in, and a session's own record names it through the session's row: that is how a session whose `claude` already exited is placed, and, on any platform where clauth cannot match a record's process (anything but Linux and macOS), every session with a clauth row; a bare `claude` session there places nothing. Its turns clear nothing either, so there a refusal a `clauth start` session's row placed stays until a later turn of a session with a clauth row clears it or a window reset leaves the account headroom. An `--isolated` run, a `clauth start --isolated` or an isolated delegate, records the refusal it ended on, and clears one on record that a later turn of the run disproves, as its tree moves into the global store, where no tree names the account any more: at its teardown, or, if its clauth died first, at the next cleanup that rescues the tree. The recovery after a switch-off reads none, so no session stays signed out once an account's windows reset.

## Choosing where a session starts

The chain decides where a session *moves*. `clauth start --auto` decides where one **starts**: it walks the fallback chain (the walk-order mode when set, chain order by default) and launches on the first member the chain itself would switch to, judged for the models the session is about to run.

**The walk is the chain's own.** The same exclusions ([below](Auto-Switch#excluded-members)) and the same lines (the 5h threshold, the weekly line, the per-model weeks) decide, in your `walk_order`: chain position by default, else the soonest weekly reset or the most weekly headroom. A member whose usage-credit cap is spent, unless it serves its own endpoint with its own key, is passed over (`credit cap spent, other models ok`) when the session will run a credit-billed model (Fable, plus any family Claude Code's flag adds), whatever its `credit gate` says. When clauth cannot tell the models — none named, one it cannot place, an agent launch that names no model, or a resume clauth cannot pin that names no model — it is passed over too, whatever the gate says, and the refusal names no model family unless the launch also holds a credit-billed model clauth can tell and no model it cannot place. A resume is judged by which transcript it runs: a resume by exact session id that clauth can pin to a top-level transcript is scanned whole, and if that transcript's last agent record names an agent the session re-runs it, so with no model named it is refused on a capped account like any agent launch. Any resume clauth cannot pin — `--continue`/`-c`, a session name, a bare `--resume`/`-r`, an id with no top-level transcript here, a nested subagent id, `-r=<id>`, `--from-pr`, `--teleport`, or `--cloud` with any value on `clauth start` — is an unknown model when none is named, and refuses on a capped account; clauth never mirrors Claude Code's transcript picker (`claude attach` and `claude respawn` reopen or restart a background session, but clauth reads no transcript for them and judges them as fresh launches). A named `--model` decides the resume alone; an explicit non-blank `--agent` you pass outranks the transcript's agent, and a blank one is ignored so the transcript's agent stands. An opus or sonnet launch still starts there. An account on record as refusing a credit-billed request ([Running it](Auto-Switch#running-it)) is passed over the same way for a launch that runs a credit-billed model or one whose model clauth cannot tell, and `--explain` says when it refused (`credits refused 2h ago`, the date from 30 days on). A member whose usage was read recently is preferred over one whose reading is stale or missing, and a chain with only stale readings still launches.

**The models are the union, never the headline model alone.** A `Task` subagent runs inside the parent's process and spends the parent's account on whatever model it runs, so judging for the main thread alone would strand the session the moment a subagent used a capped family. Each member is judged by what its own session runs. The headline is one model, by Claude Code's own precedence: the last `--model` you pass; on an agent launch with no model named — an `--agent` flag, or the settings `agent` key in any tier — clauth reads no agent definition, so it cannot tell the models at all; else the `env.ANTHROPIC_MODEL` the child sees (a `--settings` or project settings `env` value, else the runtime's — the member's `[env]`, or the shared base's `env` unless the active account's `[env]` also sets it, or any account's does while none is active — else an inherited one the runtime keeps); else the settings `model`, from a `--settings` file or inline JSON, else the project's `<cwd>/.claude/settings.local.json`, else its `<cwd>/.claude/settings.json`, else the built runtime `settings.json` (the member's `[models] default`, and a member without one has none, so the global `model` never counts). Both project tiers are read by default, narrowed to the tiers a `--setting-sources` list names, and never at your real home. Beside the headline: one fallback list, as Claude Code picks it — the last `--fallback-model` value, unless it equals the `--model` the child runs; with no flag, or one that equals it, a `--settings` `fallbackModel`, else the project's local then shared settings', else the runtime's (the global one on a shared runtime, none on an isolated one); an empty list means no fallback, and a `default` entry counts the member's `[models] default`, or, on a member without one, a model clauth cannot tell — plus `CLAUDE_CODE_SUBAGENT_MODEL` as the child sees it (a `--settings` `env` value, else the project settings', else the member's `[models] subagent` or its own `[env]`, else one inherited from your shell that the runtime keeps; a global settings one never counts). A `[models]` alias remap (`[models] opus` and friends) then replaces the alias with the `ANTHROPIC_DEFAULT_*_MODEL` model the child runs, and `best` counts as both `fable` and `opus`, `opusplan` as both `opus` and `sonnet`, while a `[1m]` suffix changes nothing. A per-model week counts only when it is one of those families; with none resolved, the blanket `scoped gate` above applies unchanged. A known model outranks the toggle: an account capped on a model this session runs is skipped even with its `scoped gate` off. When clauth cannot tell a model the session runs — an agent naming no model, a resume it cannot pin naming no model, a launch whose headline resolves to no model, or a `default` fallback entry on a member with no `[models] default` — the known families are still judged against their per-model weeks, and with the `scoped gate` on every per-model week is judged too, since the unknown model may be any family; the credit cap reads that model as unknown, and any other model clauth cannot place makes only the credit cap unknown. The walk's row names the families it judged the account on. It names none when the account was judged on the blanket gate, when the credit cap refused a launch holding a model clauth cannot place, or when clauth cannot tell a model the session runs and neither a known family's per-model week nor the credit cap on a known credit-billed family refused it. The refusal names the shared demand (every family in it) only when every judged member has the same non-empty demand.

A real launch says which account it picked on one line before the session starts. `--explain` prints the whole walk instead and exits without launching: the pick on the first line, then every chain member with the reason it was passed over (the Fallback tab's words; a credit refusal on record, which the Fallback tab does not show, reads `credits refused` with its age, as the Usage card's pill does) and how old its usage reading is: `(stale)` on a reading over two hours old that still shows a live window. An account clauth cannot measure ([above](Auto-Switch#the-decision)) and has no reading for shows `can't read usage` in place of the age, and one holding only an api key shows `api key, no usage windows`. It runs the refusals a real launch runs first, so a `--with-fallback` start that would be refused is refused here too. The readings come from each account's usage cache, which an open TUI or a running daemon keeps fresh; with neither, the age tells you how much to trust them.

```
would start on 'work' for opus + sonnet
  home   7d opus 100%, other models ok   usage 4m ago
* work   ok                              usage 4m ago
  spare  ok                              usage 3h ago (stale)
```

The candidate set is the fallback chain — the accounts you have already said may be entered unattended — so an empty chain refuses and names the fix, and so does a chain with no member left to start on; a chain naming no account that exists refuses and lists the names it holds. This never moves a running session. `--with-fallback` moves one on its own as the account runs out, and `clauth switch <sid> <profile>` moves one by hand; `--auto` and `--with-fallback` compose: pick the entry point, then let the chain rescue it if that account runs out.

## Mixing account types

A chain holding both OAuth and API-key accounts raises a confirm before it is saved. Switching away from an API-key member does not unset the environment variables a running bare `claude` already read, so that session can keep using the old endpoint until it restarts. `clauth start` sessions are unaffected.

## Codex

Codex profiles ([Codex](Codex)) have a chain of their own, and the two never mix: it is `fallback_chain` in `~/.clauth/codex-profiles.toml`, hand-edited, with the codex active marker as its anchor, its own `wrap_off`, and its own `weekly_switch_threshold` (default `98.0`, band 50-100) in place of the Config tab's `weekly limit` ([Configuration](Configuration#codex-profilestoml)). The Fallback tab and the REST chain routes edit the Claude Code chain alone.

The decision is the one above: the active codex profile has to be a chain member, it has to be exhausted or dead, and the walk takes the next member with headroom in chain order, preferring one whose usage was read live. Exhausted means the 5h window past `95%` or the weekly window past the codex line; every codex member takes those two lines and nothing else, there being no per-member card to set `rotate at`, `last resort`, `preferred`, a spend ceiling or a gate on. Dead means the chain is quarantined (`×` on the row, `broken` in the feed) or the account's usage polls keep answering 401 past two forced refreshes. Per-model weekly windows, burn-aware switching and extra usage are Claude Code concepts and do not apply.

What differs is when it lands. There is no live-session swap for codex and `--with-fallback` is refused on a codex profile: codex reads its login once at start, so a switch moves the active marker — and your own `auth.json` link with it (in `~/.codex`, or your `CODEX_HOME`), which bare `codex` reads on its next command — while a running session finishes on the account it started with and a `clauth start` session picks the switch up at its next start. codex's shared app-server daemon (codex 0.157+) holds the login it started with too; on macOS and Linux an auto-switch off a spent or dead account restarts it so the stalled tasks resume on the new one ([Codex](Codex)). The daemon log reads `clauth: codex auto-switched to '<name>'. Live at the next codex session`, or `. Restarting codex's app-server daemon so its tasks resume on it` when it restarts one. With every member spent, `wrap_off = false` stays on the last one, and `wrap_off = true` clears the active slot (`clauth: every codex account is spent. Codex active slot cleared`); a cleared slot re-arms nothing on its own, so `clauth <name>` on a codex profile is how the chain starts walking again.
